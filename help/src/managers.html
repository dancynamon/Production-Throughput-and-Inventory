Running the floor from the app

The crew lives on Floor mode. You get five tabs behind your PIN: where work is piling up, the time clock, the weekly counts, and the shelf and storage. Five minutes a day, twenty on Monday.

Log in

  1. Open the app and tap Full app (managers) at the bottom of Floor mode, or go straight to prod-through-inv-3.dan-daf.workers.dev/?full=1.

    Once you're logged in, the plain address opens the full app on that phone.

  2. Type your PIN and tap Log in.

    Either your manager PIN or the same 4-digit PIN you use on Floor mode. Two people with the same PIN are refused, so keep them different.

  3. Tap Log out at the top when you're done on a shared phone.

    Your own phone can stay logged in. If it drops you back to the login on its own, a PIN was changed: log in again.

Who is a manager. Dan, John and Alex. Dan can change the list in the sheet (Script Property MANAGERS). Manager PINs: sheet menu → Settings → Set a manager's PIN (full app).

Time clock

Floor mode's clock is the real time clock. Every clock in and every clock out needs the person's own 4-digit PIN and, once the shop location is set, the phone has to be at the shop. Open the Time tab.

  1. Set the shop location once. Stand inside the shop and tap Set shop location to where I'm standing. Check the coordinates in the box and confirm.

    Until you do, punches work from anywhere and are flagged "No location check", and a banner shows on this tab. The default radius is 150 m; change it with the SHOP_RADIUS_M script property.

  2. Give everyone a PIN. Under Clock PINs tap Set PIN beside a name. Without one, a person can't log in to Floor mode at all. Reset PIN also lifts a lockout (five wrong PINs lock a name for 15 minutes).

    PINs are stored as a salted hash. Nobody, including you, can read one back. Dan can also set them from the sheet: Settings → Set a crew clock PIN (Floor mode).

  3. Forgot-to-punch requests. Under Time requests, read the reason and tap Approve or Deny. Approving writes the punch to the time log and keeps the original values and who approved it.

  4. Edit a shift. Tap Edit on any row, change In or Out, and give a reason. The reason is required and every edit is kept in the row's history. Only managers can edit time.

6pm clock-out. Anyone still clocked in at 6pm New York time is clocked out at 6pm automatically and shows "Auto clock-out 6pm". If they really stayed later, they send "I forgot to punch" and you approve it. Someone who clocks in after 6pm is closed at 14 hours instead. Dan can move or turn off the cutoff (Script Property CLOCK_CUTOFF_HOUR).

Flags to look at: clocked in 2 hours or more with nothing logged; a shift open over 10 hours; auto clock-out at 6pm; a shift the 14-hour rule closed (any edit clears the review flag); a punch with no location check; an edited row.

Honest limits. GPS on a phone can be faked. This stops casual cheating, such as punching in from home, not a determined person.

A summary email goes out at 6pm New York time: who worked, in and out, hours, meshed, patched and boxed per person, boxed per clocked hour, open shifts, flags and pending requests. Turn it on once from the sheet: Aquamentor → Settings → Install daily 6pm time summary. It goes to the sheet owner; add more addresses in the SUMMARY_TO script property.

Reconcile: the weekly loop

If your job in the app is keeping the numbers honest, this is the only tab you need. Once a week, three counts, then a read on how the crew's entries held up against what was actually there.

  1. Count the floor.

    Opens the whole-floor walk. Every product, every station, how many are waiting. Zero is a real answer. Tick "not walked" on anything you did not get to.

  2. Count the shelf.

    Opens Inventory on the five materials the app most wants counted. Type what is there, leave the rest blank, Record.

  3. Count storage.

    Opens the finished-goods panel. What is physically boxed per product. Record.

  4. Read the cards.

    Floor: the app's pile at each station beside what you counted. Shelf: the recipe's estimate beside the count. Storage: the count, then what was made and shipped since. Anything within 10% is close. The rest is either a station someone is not logging, a recipe that is wrong, or a shipment nobody recorded.

Daily and weekly

Every day, end of shift · 5 minutes

  1. Time. Approve or deny forgot-to-punch requests. Glance at the flags, especially "nothing logged" and auto clock-outs.
  2. Floor. Where work is piling up, worst first. Anything red means five or more days to clear at the logged pace. Read the "how much to trust this" list at the bottom before you act on a pile.
  3. Summary → Fix-ups. Any entry logged twice shows here with a Reverse button. Tap it if it was a double-tap. Leave it if it was a real second batch.

Every Monday · 20 minutes

  1. Reconcile. The three counts (floor, shelf, storage) and the scorecard. Fifteen of the twenty minutes go here.
  2. Inventory → Stocktake to-do. Until it reads zero, count a few of these each week: every material never counted or below zero.
  3. Inventory → Finished goods. Count what's physically in storage per product and record it. Anything under its minimum is red; set minimums in the MinOnHand column of the sheet's FinishedGoods tab.

Reading the numbers

  • "12 short" / "5 extra." Every difference is in words, never a signed number. Short means the shelf had less than the recipe predicted. Extra means more.
  • Pace is per active day. Units divided by the days that station logged anything. Hours are thin, so units per hour is only shown where hours were entered.
  • Days to clear. What's waiting at a station divided by that station's pace. No pace yet means nobody has logged that station recently.
  • "Stages logged out of order." More units logged at a later station than at the one before it. Someone skipped a station on the phone, or the pile existed before the app did. The pile size is a guess until you walk the floor.
  • Likely duplicates. Same person, product, station, quantity and day saved more than once. The Floor tab leaves them out of the numbers. Fix-ups on Summary is where you reverse them.
  • Never counted. A material or product with no physical count yet. The first count sets the baseline. The variance on that first count means nothing; the second count is where drift becomes readable.
  • Par on Floor mode. About 1.94 boxed per clocked crew hour (320 a week over 165 crew hours). It follows the clock, so it is only as good as the punches.

Fixing entries

Crew caught it right away

Floor mode's Undo on the bottom bar, within two minutes of Submit. Materials go back too.

Anything later

Summary → Fix-ups. One Reverse button per repeated entry; it reverses the extra copies only.

Piles that don't match the floor

Reconcile → Count the floor. Counts win over the log from that moment on.

Shelf numbers that don't match

Inventory → type the real count → Record. The estimate restarts from your number and the variance is filed.

Wrong clock times

Time → Edit on the row, with a reason. The original times are kept.

Never edit the sheet's log tabs by hand. Deleting a row fixes the count but leaves the materials deducted forever. Every fix above puts things back properly.

Storage and shipping

When a tube is logged as Boxed (or a chair or mat as Box) it lands in storage automatically. It leaves storage two ways:

  • Shopify, automatically. Fulfilled orders come in on their own every hour once Dan connects the store.
  • Amazon and QuickBooks orders. Dan imports these from reports. If a shipment is already on file, importing it again changes nothing.

Produced minus shipped should equal what's in storage. When you count storage, the difference is the variance, and it's the number that tells you whether shipments are being recorded.

Counts

  1. Counts are signed with the name you logged in as.

    Yours goes on the record.

  2. Type only the numbers you actually counted.

    A blank box means "didn't count", and that material is left alone. Zero means "I looked and there's none", and that counts.

  3. Watch the difference appear as you type.

    "That can't be right" costs nothing at the shelf and a walk back from the desk.

  4. Record.

    The estimate restarts from your number. Materials that miss the same way every time are the ones whose recipe is wrong; tell Dan.

Count next on Inventory picks the five materials whose count is worth the most right now: never counted, negative, or drifting. Start there on Monday.

When something looks wrong

  • A tab says "Unknown action" or "backend is older than this app." The sheet's code is behind. It updates itself within 30 minutes of a change. Wait, then reload. If it persists, tell Dan.
  • The footer says app and backend on different versions. Normal for a few minutes after a change. Reload twice.
  • You got logged out. A PIN changed, or Dan reset something. Log in again with your PIN.
  • Someone can't log in to Floor mode. They have no clock PIN yet, or five wrong tries locked them for 15 minutes. Time → Clock PINs → Set or Reset PIN.
  • A number is negative. Materials: no opening count was ever taken. Storage: more shipped than was logged as finished. Count it, and the number resets.
  • The crew's entries look inflated. Check Summary → Fix-ups first. Then the Floor tab's trust list.
  • The crew asks what changed. The What changed link at the bottom of the full app is yours, not theirs. Tell them the crew lines in your own words.
  • Something else. Tell Dan what tab, what you tapped, and what it said. A screenshot is the fastest way.
